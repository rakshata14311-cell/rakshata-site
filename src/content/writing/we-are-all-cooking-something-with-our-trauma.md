One day, a few thousand years ago, an Indian prince sacrificed his throne and kingdom after witnessing the profound suffering of the common people during his afternoon stroll. The rest, as we know, is history. This is a real tale of enlightenment, and the moral of the story is simple: *ignorance is bliss*. **Just kidding**. The message is quite clear: as long as we are human and form an attachment to things, we will suffer.

Think about your life. How often have you come across situations that tested you? Had a loved one die? Check. Went through a heartbreak? Check. Experienced a serious mental and physical trauma? Check. This is the lived experience for most of us, for most of humanity. **Suffering is everywhere.**

Then why do we feel that we deserve special attention because we've been through something horrible? We do really seem to think that we have a patent for trauma. After indulging in actions that make our character questionable, we often attribute it to our psychological wounds. We like to think that we are special. Don't believe me?

Let's talk about the TV show *Fleabag*. The protagonist there is charming, self-hating and frequently psychopathic towards people around her. When you get near the ending, it is revealed that she was the person who had cheated with her best friend's partner, inducing her best friend's suicide. This particular plot point is presented with such grandiosity, almost justifying her present behaviour as a result of past trauma.

Suffering changes people, it transforms their psyche. There is no question about it. But is it really fair to justify the suffering of a person for all of their actions? **The audience rarely asks the question here:** is it fair to connect past trauma and future personality, is it fair to assume it to be implicit and obvious? It doesn't end with Fleabag; most plots of the novels seem to revolve around this.

We are full of complexities, quirks and acquired idiosyncrasies; of course, we are unique. All of us are, not just you or me. This is the truth, and we should be celebrating it. Enlightenment in the 21st century is not about escaping suffering; it is about embracing it to humble us.
