To quote Shakespeare: "To be or not to be."

I am convinced that we are all collectively experiencing a dark, malignant case of being bummed out all the time. Or is it just me?

So ending the misery makes sense, right? Not to be? No? No one in the crowd?

We are wired to be alive. To endure. To hope. Bla bla, I know.

We want to live in the hope of a better tomorrow. That tomorrow may never arrive though.

In fact, the better days we are desperately hoping for may already have dissolved into the void of existence. It is stuck, trying to carve its way out of the labyrinth of birth and death. There is only now — the ever-present nature of entropy actively decaying the flesh, taking the time, moving the world — being God.

I struggle quite hard to make sense of things. Of entropy. Of why it happens. What is my role in the grand scheme of things? Do I even have free will? It would be far better if someone successfully convinced me that we are all living in the Matrix — that the simulation is no joke, that it is the thing we worship, fear, respect, hate and love at the same time. I would actually give my one year's salary if someone convinced me to believe in something, you know?

I am tired of being rational; I need an imaginative opium. Religion is not my cup of tea — believe me, I tried. As much as I love to live in chaos, I cannot live with contradictions.

What would I give to experience a quiet calm? A sense of peace, a feeling of being one with the world. Presently, there is too much chaos, misery, confusion and struggle. My mind is shrouded in doubts, anticipating the wretchedness that tomorrow may bring. I fear being part of a world where I am living the same day every day — experiencing the same thing, going through this horrible, god-awful loop I know I can never get out of.

Don't be worried about my state. This is not a cry for help. Nor is this a letter announcing impending doom I am about to bring on myself. I'd hate to end life deliberately — that would be cruel and comically funny. This is me making sense of my own thoughts, trying to spot the point — the point of it all.

Tough times either make or break people. They either make one lose or find themselves. But there is no guarantee one will come out of the storm intact. I am presently of the same opinion — I do not know if I will emerge as someone who conquered her own fate, or got submerged in the afflictions that being alive brings. Time will tell if my days are numbered, or a new beginning is yet to arrive.
