Well, here's some stuff about books that will always hold a special place in my life. The habit of reading didn't come to me through some family influence or peer pressure, or for that matter, a teacher didn't shove this habit down my throat. If organic is the right word, I'd like to use it — yes, it came to me organically. I know it doesn't say much, but I did develop this habit by myself; an unending curiosity about the world is what led me to buy and read more and more books. And I am so glad I did. So, to no one in particular, here are some of the books that impacted me hugely — there are quite a few, to be honest, but these are just the sort that came to mind when I tried to write about them.

### A book that is close to my heart

Undoubtedly, *The Fault in Our Stars*. It is one of the first books I bought and read, and it's a special book for me because that's when I fell in love with words — simple, yet extraordinary. There are other books, of course, that will always hold a special place in my heart, but I cannot honestly treat them all the same. *The Fault in Our Stars* was the first book that came to mind when I heard this question, maybe because, consciously or unconsciously, this book might have had an impact on me — maybe this book is the reason I developed the habit of reading in the first place.

### A book I finished in one sitting

Oh, this is easy — the book I finished in one sitting is *Aristotle and Dante Discover the Secrets of the Universe*. One of the amazing reads, mainly because I love how the author expressed the complex and tiring vulnerabilities of characters in such a real and gentle way, and how the story progresses with characters coming to terms with their exploring sexuality, and how hard it was for them considering the story is set in a Spanish cultural background. It was truly a great read for me. In a way, it might also have helped me come to terms with how I perceive the world and myself as an individual living in it.

### Favourite book of 2020

*The Secret History*, for real — this book was just so freaking well written. The plot, the characters, the backdrop, the climax — it is just so unexpected and very dark academia, to express the book in a Gen Z way. What I loved most about the book is the students' thirst for knowledge, and a teacher who is so very simple and yet very enigmatic, full of curious mysteries, and the extent they are willing to go to learn and explore their subject, which is — drum roll — Greek philosophy. I mean, I don't really blame them, totally understandable. "Beauty is terror."

### A book that got me through something

I mean, most of the books I've read have helped me in some way or another, but if I were to point at one particular book, it's *Maybe You Should Talk to Someone* — yeah, the book is as amazing as its title. In this book, the writer, who is a doctor and therapist herself, writes down some of the real stories of her patients — interesting and impactful cases she's encountered, while remaining confidential. She also writes about what she's going through, not just in her professional career but her personal life as well. This book is incredibly well written, and how dealing with loss or pain or tragedy really depends on the individual and their experience — the book overall provides some great insight about mental health as a whole. That you are not alone, if you are going through something. It's a must-read for anyone.

### My favourite thriller

*The Silent Patient* — this is a psychological thriller. The plot sounds simple: a woman kills her husband and is admitted to a psych ward, and a psychologist develops a professional interest in her while also fighting through his own personal life. As the story slowly uncovers unexpected twists and turns, you can never guess the ending — quite a fascinating read.

### My favourite memoir

My all-time favourite memoir, forever, is *When Breath Becomes Air*, without a doubt. What a book. It made me feel emotions I had no idea I could feel so intensely. For those who don't know or have never heard of it, please google it — I can never, for the life of me, properly articulate how deep, meaningful, and surreally written this book is. I want to read it again and again until I know the words by heart, but I can never quite fight the dread of knowing the ending, and still manage to do it. Such a beautiful piece of work.
