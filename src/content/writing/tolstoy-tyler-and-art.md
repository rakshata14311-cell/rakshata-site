A quick search on Chat GPT will suggest that Art is subjective, based on each individual's perception. In normal circumstances, I would agree with that definition, or at least an attempt at the definition. But lately, with so much heated debate going on online about certain movies being termed the most progressive towards certain sexes and some extremely backwards, the overall vibe of the internet seems to dwell in these very issues.

Leo Tolstoy, one of the world's most renowned and influential authors, believes that the purpose of art is to be a universal connection of unity among individuals. Art must exist to reform humans into being more good — he goes so far as to say that without art, humanity cannot exist, or even if it does, it will be a depraved existence.

Tyler Durden from *Fight Club* is a morally grey character — he steals, he cons people, he trains people to fight brutally, but he's got a purpose for all his actions: to bring down a system of society that is unjust, corrupted and depraved. Can we forgive him for holding a guy at gunpoint, probably traumatising him for life, just to preach that life must be lived not just survived? I don't know. He is a character who eats grey colour for breakfast — it is not so easy to categorise his actions as either good or bad.

Now, if one goes with Tolstoy's definition of art, does this movie come under the category of bad art, since it so visibly propagates unlawfulness and disobedience that disturb the order of society — so obviously the "universal sense of unity among individuals" goes out the window? It does, if one looks through his perspective. But as a totality, the movie helps us understand that this kind of violence is a consequence of an inhumane capitalist society. It fed on people's desires, it manipulated them into thinking they would be more when they start to buy more, and the art of spending was turned into the crime of owning goods that did no good for us.

Tolstoy, who was religious but anarchic during his lifetime, may have thought that a life lived to serve others for the betterment of the world is a life worth living. Again, I can only say this after reading about his perspective on art.

So what place does Tyler Durden hold in Tolstoy's opinion — a villain, a hero, or just a crazy guy who needs a lot of help? One can only contemplate.
