People love to brutalise the sting of a honey bee for all the right reasons. When they attack collectively, it is quite obvious that we become a pancake. But hey, hear me out — I got stung by a single honey bee.

So it wasn't a near-death experience for me, but that one bite — or a prick, whatever you want to call it — had me develop a burning fever for nearly two days. I wasn't exactly in a lot of pain. It hurt deeply when I initially got stung, but after some time the sting was becoming less painful. I was friends with the sting, and I wanted to show off the sting at school.

### A lone bee's fascination with a kid's belly, gone wrong

I was in 4th grade. It was early in the morning, around 7 or 8 — I don't recall the time accurately — I was wearing a black shirt and shorts, walking randomly near my house. We have a lot of greenery there. Shrubs, coconut, mango, and all sorts of trees and flowering plants dwell there.

It was a school day. I have no idea what I was doing on a random morning, walking around at 7 am on a school day. But there I was. As I was walking, a bee landed on my stomach, took a big bite, and then flew away, as if this was its daily morning routine.

### The aftermath

It did not hurt — well, not immediately anyway. I stopped walking, and stood there, figuring out and dreading at the same time what to do next. Like any other kid, after the initial shock, I started freaking out, so I ran and ran until I reached my mother.

After listening to my freaking-out speech of what transpired, she asked me to lie down on the bed. I did, and she applied some ointment that cooled off the swelling. The wound wasn't big. It looked like a snake bite — a dot, vibrant red — but it stung like a motherf— sorry, it stung very badly indeed. But the ointment was like the elixir that soothed my burning skin; it helped me fall asleep soon.

### Doctor who?

While I might have reminisced about the pleasure of missing school in my sleep, I woke up to the scene of my mother checking my forehead for a fever. It was pretty hot. To this day I never know what exactly triggered that fever, because my mother decided against seeing a doctor.

I was made to take random tablets that a curious neighbour suggested, and a cold, damp cloth was put on my forehead. I fell asleep again. This process happened on and off for a day and a half. The fever finally faded away — I could only see the red dot, but there was no pain.

### My regret for not having searing pain and a horrible wound

My mother thanked my neighbour and invited her for lunch. While we were helping ourselves to the food, she told me with a wink, *"Make sure you exaggerate a lot when you share this story with your friends at school."*

I did not say anything, but wished only that the dot was bigger and more awful, to corroborate those exaggerations.
