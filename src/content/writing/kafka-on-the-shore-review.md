*Kafka on the Shore* isn't an easy book to review. Mainly because, if one is steeped in the ritual of reading this book, it is hard to point out where reality starts and dream stops. The book is, I feel, an attempt to unearth the wavering complexities of life, philosophy, nature, human emotions and magical surrealism.

Reading *Kafka on the Shore* was indeed a unique experience, one that probably cannot be replaced. Not to sound too forward, but I cannot go back to the person I was before reading this particular book. I felt the unaccustomed serendipity of coming across a Murakami work and reading it in one sitting — to truly experience an adventure that I was on, in some far-away misty world, or was I merely high on some hallucination-inducing drug, I cannot tell.

**Coming to the plot** — it revolves around a 15-year-old teenager, Kafka Tamura, who seeks answers from a dark prophecy made by his villainous father (who is also a cat killer, by the way), and invariably encounters passing forms of solitude and an old man named Nakata, whose childlike innocence involves a cataclysmic mystery he seeks to find. How the characters' stories intertwine with each other to make a fantastic blend of compelling narratives is indeed worth mentioning.

There are various bits and pieces about solitude acting as a powerful medicine, cats always being the zen masters at looking at things from a wiser perspective and indulging in passing advice to naive characters, and books which act as a portal to exploring parallel and mysterious worlds filled with erotic passion and unending violence, blending the differences of past and present.

Murakami has created a brilliant piece of work, which is of course not completely devoid of flaws. Here is one of my favourite quotes of his:

> "I have no great explanation for it, but one thing I can say. Works that have a certain imperfection to them have an appeal for that very reason — or at least they appeal to certain types of people."

Reading a book of this narrative may not be everybody's cup of tea, and if you are one of those individuals who always prefers a "proper and conventional ending" that pleases your rational mind, then *Kafka on the Shore* is definitely not for you. This story, this piece of Murakami's work, looks beyond the conventional narrative one seems to ingest on a daily basis.
